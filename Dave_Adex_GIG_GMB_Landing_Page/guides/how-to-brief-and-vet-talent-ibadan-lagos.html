How to Brief and Vet Talent in Ibadan & Lagos

Give a talent broker enough context to build a useful shortlist without overpromising a booking.

A talent search usually becomes difficult before anyone discusses a rate. If the brief does not explain the outcome, timing, location, usage and working conditions, even a good shortlist can be the wrong shortlist. This guide gives buyers in Ibadan and Lagos a practical way to prepare the information a talent broker needs.

Short answer

Start with the work, not a vague job title. State what the person must do, where and when it will happen, what evidence of experience matters, how the work will be used and what budget or approval process applies.

1. Write the brief around the outcome

“I need a model” is not enough information for a commercial casting request. Explain whether the person is appearing in a product shoot, launch, social video, conference, performance or another project. Add the audience and the action the talent must perform so the shortlist is based on fit rather than appearance alone.

For Ibadan and Lagos work, include the exact venue or area when known, the expected call time, the number of hours, wardrobe or grooming expectations, language needs and whether the project is live, recorded or both.

2. Separate evidence from guarantees

A responsible vetting process can review a portfolio, references, identity information and relevant work evidence. It can make the introduction better documented, but it cannot guarantee that a person will perform perfectly, remain available or be suitable for every working environment.

Ask what was actually checked and what remains for the buyer to confirm. If the project involves minors, sensitive information, travel or high-value equipment, agree on the appropriate checks and permissions before a booking is accepted.

3. Make the local handoff practical

A Lagos production may involve traffic, long call times, equipment movement and a large crew. An Ibadan booking may have different venue access, transport and supplier arrangements. Put those practical details in the brief instead of leaving them for the talent to guess after the introduction.

Dave Adex can coordinate the shortlist, availability conversation and next step. The final work agreement should still state the parties, deliverables, payment terms, usage and cancellation expectations clearly.

Brief checklist

  • ✓Project outcome and role
  • ✓Date, call time and venue area
  • ✓Usage, audience and deliverables
  • ✓Evidence or experience required
  • ✓Budget, approval route and contact person

Questions this guide answers

What should a talent brief contain before a shortlist is requested?

Include the project outcome, role, date, call time, location, audience, deliverables, usage, budget range and decision-maker.

How can a buyer compare talent without rushing into a booking?

Review evidence, availability, communication fit and the written scope together; a profile alone is not a performance guarantee.

Can a local Ibadan or Lagos project be coordinated remotely?

Yes, when the brief, availability checks and handoff are clear. Venue access, transport and call-time details still need local confirmation.
