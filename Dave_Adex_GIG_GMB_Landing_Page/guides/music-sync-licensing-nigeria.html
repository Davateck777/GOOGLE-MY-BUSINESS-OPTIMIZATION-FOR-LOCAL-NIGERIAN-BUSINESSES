Music and Sync Licensing: A Practical Guide for Nigerian Projects

Make a music licensing brief easier to review by separating the composition, master recording, usage and approval requirements.

A beat or song can sound perfect for an advert, film, podcast or social campaign and still be unusable until the relevant rights are clear. A licensing conversation becomes faster when the buyer and rights holder can see exactly what is being requested.

Short answer

Identify the composition and master owners, samples or third-party elements, media channels, territory, term, exclusivity, edits, deliverables and fee structure before treating a track as cleared.

Separate the rights before discussing the fee

A musical work and a sound recording are related but not identical rights. A buyer may need permission for the composition and the specific master recording, especially when a track will be placed under video, advertising or branded content.

Ask who owns or controls each part. If there are co-writers, a label, samples, featured performers or a producer agreement, record those parties before a commercial use is promised.

Write the sync brief like a production brief

State the project, scene or campaign, duration of use, territory, platforms, paid-media use, term, edits, exclusivity, release date and budget. An artist can then decide whether the opportunity matches the track and the rights they control.

For a Nigerian campaign targeting Ibadan, Lagos or a wider market, specify whether the buyer needs local digital use, national broadcast, global social distribution or an internal-only licence. “For a video” is too broad to approve responsibly.

Keep the handoff documented

Use clean filenames and metadata, keep instrumental and clean versions separate, and record who approved the final file. A short written licence summary should identify the parties, permitted use, fee, term and delivery obligations.

Dave Adex can coordinate artist, producer, buyer and rights-holder conversations. The final licence should be reviewed by the relevant parties or qualified advisers; no marketplace introduction is itself a rights clearance.

Brief checklist

  • ✓Composition and master owners
  • ✓Samples, performers and co-writers
  • ✓Media, territory and term
  • ✓Exclusivity and edit permissions
  • ✓Files, metadata and approval trail

Questions this guide answers

Which rights need attention before music is used in an advert or video?

Check the musical composition, master recording, samples, performers, publishers and any territory, term or media restrictions that apply.

Is a beat licence automatically the same as sync permission?

No. A beat or recording licence may not cover the composition, audiovisual placement, edits, territory or commercial campaign use.

What should an artist send with a licensing submission?

Send the track, instrumental or clean version where relevant, metadata, ownership information, contact route and a clear statement of available rights.
