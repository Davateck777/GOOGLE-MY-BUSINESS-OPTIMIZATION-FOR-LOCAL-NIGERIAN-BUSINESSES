How to Brief and Supervise a Social Media Manager in Ibadan & Lagos

Separate content production, publishing, community management and paid advertising so the manager has a workable brief.

A social media manager cannot be accountable for a result that was never defined. Local businesses often combine content, customer replies, design, ad buying and sales follow-up in one sentence. A better brief separates the work and gives the manager an approval route.

Short answer

Define platforms, audience, offer, posting rhythm, asset supply, response hours, escalation rules, approval time and reporting metrics before hiring or supervising a manager.

Define the actual role

Decide whether the client needs a content planner, copywriter, designer, videographer, publisher, community manager, paid-media operator or a supervised combination. A person can cover several roles, but the client should still describe each responsibility.

For a business serving Ibadan and Lagos, include the questions customers ask in each market, the locations or delivery areas that matter, the offers that are actually available and the person who confirms prices or stock.

Build an approval and response workflow

Agree on content themes, formats, posting rhythm, asset deadlines and approval time. If the brand takes messages, define who responds, what can be answered immediately and what must be escalated to the owner or operations team.

Meta’s business tools can centralise some messages and follow-up tasks, but software does not replace a clear human owner. A manager should not invent stock, prices, availability, medical claims or review responses.

Measure useful work

Choose metrics that match the brief: qualified enquiries, response time, content completion, saves, shares, profile visits, booked calls or campaign actions. Follower count alone rarely explains whether the local business is receiving useful attention.

Dave Adex can place or supervise a social media manager and coordinate the handoff with GBP, PR, paid campaigns or content production. The scope should say what is included and what remains the client’s responsibility.

Brief checklist

  • ✓Platforms and audience
  • ✓Role split: content, publishing, community, ads
  • ✓Asset and approval workflow
  • ✓Response hours and escalation rules
  • ✓Metrics and reporting cadence

Questions this guide answers

What should a local brand put in a social media manager brief?

Name the platforms, audience, offer, content formats, posting rhythm, response hours, approval owner, escalation rules and reporting expectations.

How do client approvals affect the content workflow?

Set a deadline and one decision-maker for approvals; otherwise production stalls and the manager cannot keep a reliable publishing rhythm.

Is content production the same as community management?

No. Creating posts, publishing them, replying to people, moderating comments and handling leads are different tasks that should be scoped separately.
