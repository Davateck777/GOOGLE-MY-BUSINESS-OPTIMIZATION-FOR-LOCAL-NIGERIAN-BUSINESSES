How to Brief Commercial Photography and Media Production in Ibadan & Lagos

Define the deliverables, usage, production conditions and post-production needs before booking a photographer or media team.

A commercial photo or media brief should make the finished deliverables visible before the camera is booked. Clients often know the campaign idea but leave the format, usage, production conditions and revision expectations unclear. That is where avoidable cost and delay begin.

Short answer

List the audience, shot list, output formats, location, talent, equipment, deadline, usage rights, retouching or editing requirements, approval process and the person responsible for sign-off.

Describe the finished asset

Say where the image or video will appear: product page, social campaign, press release, billboard, event screen, internal presentation or paid advertising. Then state the aspect ratios, resolution, duration, captions, audio and number of final assets required.

A mood board is useful, but it does not replace a shot list. Include must-have frames, optional frames, product details, people required and any brand rules the crew must follow.

Plan the production conditions

Confirm location, access, power, weather backup, parking, equipment, crew size, talent, wardrobe, makeup, sound and schedule. A shoot in Ibadan and a shoot in Lagos may have different transport and supplier coordination needs, even when the creative brief is similar.

If the client needs both stills and video, say so at the beginning. The lighting, audio, production design and schedule may need to support both outputs. A photographer working alone should not be expected to deliver a full film crew’s scope without a realistic plan.

Protect the post-production handoff

Agree on file delivery, backup, selection process, colour direction, retouching, captions, animation, revision rounds and final approval. Make it clear who supplies logos, product files, music and legal usage approvals.

Questions this guide answers

What should a commercial photography brief include?

Include the campaign objective, shot list, location, talent, mood references, deliverable formats, usage, schedule, post-production and approval route.

How do clients separate shoot cost from editing cost?

Treat capture, equipment, crew, retouching, video editing, animation, travel and licensing as visible scope lines rather than one unexplained number.

When does a brand need a full production team?

Use a larger team when the brief involves multiple locations, talent, lighting, audio, set design, video, tight turnaround or complex approvals.
